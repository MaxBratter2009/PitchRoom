Pieces to the puzzle

The Pitch Room isn't about the highlight reel. It's about the in-between — the pivots, the funding rounds that almost didn't close, the products that nearly killed the company, and the moments when quitting felt like the only rational choice.

Each episode, host Max Bratter sits down with a founder or operator who shares one piece of the entrepreneurship puzzle — their chapter of the story, told in the city where they built it.

Over time, episode by episode, the full picture comes together.

Cities We Visit
Episode City · East Coast

New York City

Finance, media, fashion, tech. NYC founders operate on a different frequency — high stakes, high density, relentless competition. The city that never lets you coast.

Episode City · South

Houston

Energy, space, healthcare, and a bootstrapping ethos that runs deep. Houston entrepreneurs build differently — bigger in scope, quieter in ego. The underrated startup capital.

Episode City · West Coast

San Francisco

The valley and the bay — where the biggest bets get made. Venture capital, moonshots, and the culture of building something that doesn't exist yet. Risk as a default setting.

One piece at a time. The whole puzzle reveals itself.

Every founder has a chapter. A hard-won lesson. A specific moment that changed everything. The Pitch Room extracts that chapter — one conversation at a time — until the full journey of entrepreneurship comes into view.

Your Host

Max
Bratter

Max Bratter is a young entrepreneur, nonprofit founder, and broadcaster who understands — better than most — that the path to doing something meaningful is long, winding, and entirely worth it.

As founder and station manager of WBIT — one of fewer than five public radio stations in the country run entirely by a high school — Max produces live morning shows, news coverage, and now, The Pitch Room. Through NSU University School's USchool Network, he has built a track record in storytelling, interviewing, and live production that translates directly into what this show is trying to do.

He also leads Task Troopers, a 501(c)(3) he built from the ground up, and competes in VEX Robotics at the national level. He started all of it before college.
